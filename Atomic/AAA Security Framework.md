The **AAA security framework**: **Assurance, Authenticity, and Anonymity**. complements the CIA triad by addressing trust, origin verification, and identity protection in computing systems.

## Assurance
Establishes confidence that a system operates as intended and that users or entities are who they claim to be. It is supported through security policies, access permissions, authentication mechanisms, and technical controls.

## Authenticity
Verifies that information, actions, policies, or communications genuinely originate from their claimed source and have not been altered. Digital signatures and cryptographic hashing are common mechanisms used to support authenticity and nonrepudiation.

## Anonymity
Protects identity by preventing actions, requests, or transactions from being linked to a specific individual. Techniques such as proxies, pseudonyms, aggregation, mixing, and onion routing can be used to provide anonymity.

---

Together, Assurance, Authenticity, and Anonymity provide a framework for reasoning about **whether systems can be trusted, whether information genuinely comes from its claimed source, and whether identities can remain protected**.
